The Importance of Airline Data

Civil aviation data is one of the most underutilized yet powerful lenses for understanding global stability, economic resilience, and the hidden costs of conflict.

Why Airline Data Matters

Commercial aircraft are constrained by geopolitics in real time. Unlike maritime or ground logistics, air routes react almost immediately to conflict, sanctions, and diplomatic breakdowns. A single airspace closure can ripple across continents within hours.

By observing deviations in flight paths, changes in density, and temporal disruptions, airline data becomes a proxy signal for:

  • Escalation or de-escalation of conflicts
  • Economic friction and fuel inefficiency
  • Pressure on neighboring airspaces
  • Environmental externalities caused by rerouting

Aviation as a Global System

The global aviation network behaves like a tightly coupled system. Local disruptions propagate non-linearly, affecting airlines, airports, air traffic controllers, fuel suppliers, and downstream logistics.

Studying aviation data therefore enables analysis beyond transportation — it opens a window into how modern globalization absorbs shocks.

From Routes to Reality

Every rerouted flight encodes a decision influenced by safety advisories, insurance risk, geopolitical alliances, and economic trade-offs. At scale, these decisions form observable patterns that can be measured and compared.

How We Use OpenSky Data

This project leverages historical and near-real-time aircraft surveillance data provided by the OpenSky Network. OpenSky aggregates ADS-B transmissions from a global network of sensors, enabling independent and transparent access to civil aviation movements.

Specifically, we utilize OpenSky data to:

  • Extract flight trajectories across predefined time windows
  • Compare peace-time and conflict-time routing behavior
  • Generate density and heat maps over major regions
  • Correlate route deviations with conflict timelines

All processing is performed via reproducible notebooks, ensuring transparency and methodological clarity.

Data Ethics and Limitations

While airline data is powerful, it is not neutral. Coverage gaps, sensor density, reporting biases, and regulatory constraints can influence visibility. This project explicitly documents assumptions, limitations, and uncertainties in every analysis.

Our intent is not surveillance, but understanding — using open data responsibly to study large-scale systemic effects of conflict.
